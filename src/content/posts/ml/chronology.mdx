#

## 1895 – A Humble beginning in a deeply rooted faith

* Born on February 25, 1895, in Kuravilangad, Travancore, into the ancient Pakalomattom-Parekunnel family, tracing its Christian roots to the earliest converts by St. Thomas the Apostle.
* Father was Joseph Nedumattam originally of Pakalomattom family of Kuravilangad and mother was Eliamma of Parekunnel House

## Early Education

* Early education started at Kuravilangad Church Pallikudam founded by Nidhirikkal Mani Kathanar
* Matriculated with first class marks from St. Ephraim's High School, Mannanam
* Got 1st class in intermediate from C.M.S. College of Kottayam

## - 1924 - A Deeply Brilliant Scholar

* B.A. (Hons.) in History and Economics from St. Joseph’s College, Trichy (Recipient of Norton Prize and the Raja Sir Ramaswami Gold Medal, 1916).
* Completed his B.Litt. and D.Phil. in Economics from Balliol College, Oxford University, among the first Indian economists to earn a doctorate from Oxford.
* His B.Litt. research focused on the Indian calico trade and became a foundation for later studies in colonial trade and mercantilism.

# Family

* He married Annamma Ponnatt of Purappuzha (Thodupuzha) and had two daughters Alice and Rosamma and one son Joseph over the following years

## 1924–1942 – Professor, Prolific Writer, Reformer

* Was lecturer at the Ceylon University College, Colombo (1924-27). Then became professor at the University of Madras, where he headed the Economics Department.
* Authored pioneering works on rural indebtedness, cooperative credit, and federal finance.
* His book The Growth of Federal Finance in India (1939) became a cornerstone of Indian fiscal thought.
* Conducted rural economic surveys in South India, gathering systematic data on poverty and debt through on-site field work, allowing policymakers to understand rural challenges with real data.

## 1937 onwards – Economic Statesmanship

* Elected President of the Indian Economic Association
* Nominated to the Madras Legislative Council.
* Economic Adviser to the Madras State under C. Rajagopalachari. During the prohibition experiment in Salem he introduced the concept of sales tax — a model later adopted nationwide.

## 1942–1948 – Architect of Post-War Indian Economic Thinking

* Appointed Economic Adviser to the Government of India — first under British rule, then under Independent India in the Jawaharlal Nehru Government.
* Championed rural employment and cooperative movements.

## 1944-1945 – Represented India on the Global stage

* Attended landmark international events which resulted in the birth of the post World War 2 world order
* Delegate at the San Francisco Conference, 1945, which adopted the United Nations Charter. Was a key member of the sub committee (committee 3) which drafted the framework of the third organ of the UN, that is, the ECOSOC Council.

Delegates of fifty nations met at San Francisco between April 25 and June 26, 1945. Working on the Dumbarton Oaks proposals, the Yalta Agreement, and amendments proposed by various Governments, the Conference agreed upon the Charter of the United Nations and the Statute of the new International Court of Justice. The Charter was passed unanimously and signed by all the representatives. It came into force on October 24, 1945, when China, France, the USSR, the United Kingdom, and the United States and a majority of the other signatories had filed their instruments of ratification.

## 1950–1952 – Builder of Institutions and Mentor

* Returned to Kerala to become the founding principal of St. Thomas College, Palai — blending academic excellence with moral formation.

## 1957–1962 – A Parliamentary Voice for the Poor

* Nominated to the Rajya Sabha from the State of Kerala by both the ruling Communist Party of India and the Congress. During his tenure he championed for land reform, decentralized planning and employment for the rural poor.
